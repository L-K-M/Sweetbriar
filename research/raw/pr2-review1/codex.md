**(a) GOA OAuth reuse only with GNOME's agreement — my view:** keep this release gate, while accurately distinguishing it from a technical API restriction. [GNOME's policy](https://gnome.pages.gitlab.gnome.org/gnome-online-accounts/goals.html) asks for consultation and protection of third-party identity. A written integration arrangement should establish consent-screen attribution, permitted consumers, scopes and responsibility for policy compliance. GNOME's agreement does not itself transfer Google's verification or override tenant consent; [Google's OAuth policy](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/policies), last modified **2026-08-05**, requires appropriate registration and accurate identity, and its [restricted-scope guidance](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification) defines additional review requirements. The supported provider arrangement remains **unverified** for Sweetbriar. Keep own registrations as the baseline and fallback; never copy GNOME's client identity into Sweetbriar's authorization flow.

The strongest counter-argument is that GOA deliberately offers a consumer API, and [Geary already obtains tokens through it](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GNOME/geary/main/src/client/application/goa-mediator.vala). Requiring a bespoke agreement can delay a native Linux convenience feature without adding a technical security boundary. That argues for early consultation and a supported broker arrangement, rather than abandoning integration.

**(b) Opaque compose identity strip — my view:** use it as the default, with the full From address and a saturated marker. It offers a controlled surface for contrast calculations and a consistent location across platforms. This fits Apple's requirement to [provide non-color information and appearance variants](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color), and its instruction to [avoid translucent backgrounds under Reduce Transparency](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsworkspace/accessibilitydisplayshouldreducetransparency). Keep Strong tint optional and retain the neutral editor. Opacity still requires testing actual foregrounds, inactive appearance, enlarged text and full screen; it cannot substitute for identity validation or the frozen send route.

The strongest counter-argument is that a restrained strip may be too subtle for peripheral recognition, while tinted glass better matches native chrome and a tinted upper window makes simultaneous accounts easier to distinguish. Apple's [toolbar guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars) discourages excessive custom backgrounds, but does not prove this strip optimal. Compare Standard and Strong with users; change the default if measured recognition improves without readability/accessibility regressions.
