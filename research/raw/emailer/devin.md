# Claris Emailer — research report for Sweetbriar

*Research compiled 2026-10-11. Facts verified against sources linked inline; recollections and unverified claims are marked. Time-sensitive facts are dated "as of 2026-10-11".*

## 1. TL;DR

- Claris Emailer (1995–1998) was a Mac-only email client created by **Fog City Software** (Guy Kawasaki's company; lead developer **Jud Spencer**) and marketed by Apple's subsidiary Claris. Its defining idea: **one client, one inbox, one outbox, one address book, one rule engine across every mail service you used** — POP3/SMTP internet mail, AOL, CompuServe, eWorld, AppleLink, RadioMail, and later Claris OfficeMail ([TidBITS, 1995](https://tidbits.com/1995/04/03/emailer-licensed-by-claris/); [Lite manual](https://archive.org/details/claris-emailer-lite-manual)).
- It was **offline-first by design for dial-up**: compose and file everything locally, then a "connection" batch-send/received across all services, manually or on **Schedules** (timed connections, repeating intervals, per-account selection, per-location dial profiles) ([TidBITS 2.0 review](https://tidbits.com/1997/06/02/email-reliance-emailer-2-0/); [Low End Mac](https://lowendmac.com/hints/emailer.shtml)).
- The power trio devotees still miss in 2026: **Mail Actions** (filters that could also auto-reply and auto-forward, with criteria on address-book/group membership), **keyboard-driven filing** (Cmd-Opt-F, type the folder name), and **automatic filing after read/send/reply** plus **auto-expiry of read/sent/deleted mail** ([TidBITS 2000](https://tidbits.com/2000/04/24/switching-from-emailer-to-eudora-in-leaps-and-bounds/); [Entourage blog comment, recollection](https://web.archive.org/web/20080624084550/http://blog.entourage.mvps.org/2007/05/in_the_beginning.html); [Lite manual §A-2](https://archive.org/details/claris-emailer-lite-manual)).
- Its two big weaknesses were also storage decisions: **1.x stored each message as a Finder file** (huge HFS block waste), and **2.0 switched to one monolithic Mail Database + Mail Index** — faster and smaller, but prone to corruption, painful to back up/sync, and hard to leave ([TidBITS 1995](https://tidbits.com/1995/07/31/claris-emailer-ships/); [TidBITS 2000](https://tidbits.com/2000/04/24/switching-from-emailer-to-eudora-in-leaps-and-bounds/)).
- It was **deeply scriptable**: first-class AppleScript support from v1.0v2, an AppleScript menu, and a community script ecosystem (Fog City's utilities page, Dan Crevier's scripts, the EmailTools palette) that filled almost every product gap ([Fog City utilities](http://www.fogcity.com/em_utilities2.0.html); [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- Killed in the January 1998 Claris→FileMaker Inc. restructuring; the team rushed out **2.0v3** (certified ~Jan 22, 1998) as the final release, then **the core developers — Spencer, Dan Crevier, Dave Cortright, Dave Marandino — went to Microsoft** and built Outlook Express for Mac, which became Entourage, then Outlook for Mac ([TidBITS 1998](https://tidbits.com/1998/02/16/emailers-last-gasp-update-2/); [Entourage blog, recollection](https://web.archive.org/web/20080624084550/http://blog.entourage.mvps.org/2007/05/in_the_beginning.html)).
- Worth reviving in Sweetbriar: unified multi-backend inbox as an *adapter architecture*, a **reviewable outbox queue with per-recipient/per-message control**, Mail Actions as a **local rules engine (optionally synced to Sieve/Gmail filters/Graph rules)**, named+colored priority labels mapped to IMAP keywords, client-side retention policies, the **Auto File Log** audit view, and serious scriptability (Shortcuts/App Intents + CLI). Not worth reviving: monolithic opaque storage, modal connect dialogs, and proprietary-service plumbing.
- Timely warning: **EWS is being blocked in Exchange Online starting 2026-10-01, fully retired by 2027-04** — Sweetbriar's Exchange support must be Microsoft Graph, not EWS ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/exchange/clients-and-mobile-in-exchange-online/deprecation-of-ews-exchange-online)).

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 History

**Origin.** Emailer was created by **Fog City Software**, the company Guy Kawasaki founded after leaving Apple. Fog City was a small virtual company; contemporaneous reporting credits Emailer to Fog City with development led by **Jud Spencer** (working from Texas), alongside Fog City staff Will Mayall, Kathryn Henkens, and Tom Trefny ([TidBITS "Claris Emailer Ships," 1995-07-31](https://tidbits.com/1995/07/31/claris-emailer-ships/); [Fog City](http://www.fogcity.com/)). A later recollection adds that Spencer was already known for TouchBase, a well-regarded contact manager ([Entourage blog comment](https://web.archive.org/web/20080624084550/http://blog.entourage.mvps.org/2007/05/in_the_beginning.html)).

**Claris deal.** On 1995-04-03 TidBITS reported that Claris had **licensed** Emailer: Claris would market it while Fog City continued development ([TidBITS "Emailer Licensed by Claris"](https://tidbits.com/1995/04/03/emailer-licensed-by-claris/)). **Claris Emailer 1.0 shipped in July 1995** ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/1995/07/31/claris-emailer-ships/)). A 2007 Entourage-community history claims Claris *purchased* Emailer in May 1996 and released 1.0 in June 1996 — that conflicts with the contemporaneous TidBITS timeline and should be treated as a recollection error on dates (the team/purchase gist is plausible) ([Entourage blog](https://web.archive.org/web/20080624084550/http://blog.entourage.mvps.org/2007/05/in_the_beginning.html)). Fog City's own site says simply: "Emailer was created by Fog City Software. Apple owns Emailer" ([fogcity.com](http://www.fogcity.com/)).

A **Windows version was announced for 1997 but development never actually started**, per the Entourage blog history ([recollection](https://web.archive.org/web/20080624084550/http://blog.entourage.mvps.org/2007/05/in_the_beginning.html)). Ports to other platforms never existed.

**Business story and death.** Emailer won real acclaim — 1997 Macworld Eddy Award for Best Internet Client Software, a 1996 USENET Macintosh Programming Award for Spencer, and Editors' Choice mentions (per the Entourage blog's award list; awards corroborated in part by [Low End Mac](https://lowendmac.com/1998/can-apple-save-emailer-by-giving-it-away/)). But in late January 1998 Apple restructured Claris into **FileMaker, Inc.**, killing or divesting everything except FileMaker and Home Page; ~300 Claris staff were laid off ([TidBITS "Claris to Restructure as FileMaker, Inc."](https://tidbits.com/1998/02/02/claris-to-restructure-as-filemaker-inc-2/)). Emailer 2.0v3 was reportedly certified the day before the rename — a commenter on the Entourage blog recalls developers and beta testers "worked their butts off during the final days" to get it out ([recollection](https://web.archive.org/web/20080624084550/http://blog.entourage.mvps.org/2007/05/in_the_beginning.html)). Apple never sold or open-sourced Emailer despite community lobbying ([Low End Mac, 1998](https://lowendmac.com/1998/can-apple-save-emailer-by-giving-it-away/)). A persistent rumor held that Apple sacrificed Emailer for Microsoft's Outlook Express as part of the 1997 Apple–Microsoft deal; **unverified** — no primary evidence found ([Low End Mac](https://lowendmac.com/1998/can-apple-save-emailer-by-giving-it-away/)).

**Team and code lineage.** The code died with the product (proprietary, never released). The *team* went to Microsoft: per Diane Ross's Entourage Help Blog, **Jud Spencer, Dan Crevier (Emailer's AppleScript implementer), Dave Cortright, and Dave Marandino** joined Microsoft's Mac Business Unit; Outlook Express for Mac development began May 1997 under Dick Craddock, OE shipped ~1998, was renamed Entourage (first beta "alpaca"; released 2000 as part of Office 2001), and became Outlook for Mac in 2011 ([Entourage blog](https://web.archive.org/web/20080624084550/http://blog.entourage.mvps.org/2007/05/in_the_beginning.html); corroborated in spirit by [TidBITS 2023 comments](https://tidbits.com/2023/03/08/microsoft-outlook-for-mac-now-free-with-strings-attached/)). This is community-sourced lineage — treat names/dates as recollection, though it is the best available account and is consistent with Microsoft's product history.

**Afterlife.** Emailer kept working in Mac OS 9 and the Classic environment; one user reports running it daily until December 2008, when his Classic-capable machine was retired ([blog](http://gettheflick.blogspot.com/2008/12/goodbye-faithful-servant.html)). The **Emailer-talk** mailing list (eskimo.com/SkyTouch, later Yahoo Groups) survived for years; Wikipedia reports it still active (date of claim not verified). Fog City still hosts Emailer files and utilities ([fogcity.com](http://www.fogcity.com/em_utilities2.0.html), retrieved 2026-10-11).

### 2.2 Feature inventory

Primary source: *Claris Emailer User's Guide* (Lite edition, ©1995 Claris/Fog City; [scanned manual](https://archive.org/details/claris-emailer-lite-manual), local OCR `sources/emailer-lite-manual.txt`). Cited below as **LM** with chapter/appendix; note the OCR has artifacts. 1.x full and 2.x deltas from release notes and community documentation are labeled.

**Accounts and services**
- Services supported: Internet (POP3/SMTP, MacTCP/Open Transport; optional APOP), America Online, CompuServe, eWorld, RadioMail; install also shipped an AppleLink folder and 2.0 added Claris OfficeMail (LM ch. 1; [TidBITS review](https://tidbits.com/1997/06/02/email-reliance-emailer-2-0/); [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)). It was reportedly **the only third-party client licensed to access AOL email directly** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claris_Emailer); community corroboration: [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- Up to five accounts in full 1.x; Lite = one Internet account only (LM ch. 1; [CEMH](https://web.archive.org/web/1999/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/version.shtml)).
- Per-service and per-account options: "Leave mail on server" for N days, "Ignore already read messages", partial retrieve ("Partially retrieve messages larger than…" — headers first, fetch the rest on demand or nuke it), "Get all messages from server" (full re-fetch), password optional per session (LM; [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml); [2.0v2 readme](http://download.info.applefritter.com/Apple_Support_Area/Apple_Software_Updates/English-North_American/Macintosh/Application_Updates/Claris_Emailer/Claris_Emailer_2.x/Emailer2.0to2.0v2__Info.txt)).
- CompuServe could ride either dial-up or a TCP connection via third-party Comm Toolbox telnet tools (Black Knight, TCPack); AOL/eWorld had included TCP tools; CompuServe "surcharge warnings" could be suppressed (LM; [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml); [Wired](https://www.wired.com/1996/02/all-in-one-e-mail/)).

**Connections, locations, schedules (offline-first dial-up core)**
- "Connect Now" dialog chose which services/accounts to poll; "Connect Again" repeated the last settings (Cmd-Opt-K); Send Now on an open message sent *only that message*, while Option-click opened send options ([CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- Connection Status window showed per-message progress: subject of the current message, throughput estimate, "no mail waiting"; Stop button, Option-Stop = stop all pending connections ([CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml); [2.0v2 readme](http://download.info.applefritter.com/Apple_Support_Area/Apple_Software_Updates/English-North_American/Macintosh/Application_Updates/Claris_Emailer/Claris_Emailer_2.x/Emailer2.0to2.0v2__Info.txt)).
- **Schedules**: named schedules connecting selected accounts at chosen times/days or repeating every N minutes; required Emailer to be running (LM index ~line 7729; [Low End Mac](https://lowendmac.com/hints/emailer.shtml); [TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/1997/06/02/email-reliance-emailer-2-0/)).
- **Locations**: per-location connection profiles (different dial-in numbers/settings) selected before connecting (LM; [TidBITS "Ships"](https://tidbits.com/1995/07/31/claris-emailer-ships/)).

**Mail storage and folders**
- 1.x: every message a separate file inside `Claris Emailer Files` → In Box/Out Box/Filing Cabinet/etc. Finder folders (LM ch. 1, file layout).
- 2.0: single **Mail Database + Mail Index** files; Option-launch rebuild (Typical = purge deleted + compact; Advanced = regenerate index, un-deletes; auto-backup made in Mail folder) ([2.0v2 readme](http://download.info.applefritter.com/Apple_Support_Area/Apple_Software_Updates/English-North_American/Macintosh/Application_Updates/Claris_Emailer/Claris_Emailer_2.x/Emailer2.0to2.0v2__Info.txt); [CEMH corruption FAQ](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/most.shtml)).
- Hierarchical folders to five levels in 2.0, relocatable by drag-and-drop in 2.0v3, full paths in filing pop-ups, pseudo-hierarchical "Folder->Sub" menu names; folders could be hidden from the pop-up ("Show in popup menu") and the browser could show only marked folders ([Claris product page](https://web.archive.org/web/1997/http://www.claris.com/products/claris/emailer/emailer.html); [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- Special folders: In Box, Out Box, Sent Mail, Read Mail, Deleted Mail (two-stage delete — the 2.0v3 warning only fired on permanent deletes), Logs, and the **Auto File Log** — a durable log listing everything the Mail Actions filed, with the destination folder as a column; entries persist even after messages move/delete ([CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml); [CEMH Mail Actions](https://web.archive.org/web/1999/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/mailactions.shtml)).

**Browser and message handling**
- Single "browser" window: folder list + message list panes; sortable/resizable columns, click column headers to sort, "sort pyramid" toggles direction; sort by read/replied status via the envelope column; new mail at top (LM ch. 2; [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml); [TidBITS 2000](https://tidbits.com/2000/04/24/switching-from-emailer-to-eudora-in-leaps-and-bounds/)).
- Search/find within folders; 2.0 could run simultaneous searches ([TidBITS review](https://tidbits.com/1997/06/02/email-reliance-emailer-2-0/)).
- **Priorities**: user-renamable, colored priority labels applied manually or by Mail Action; sortable Priority column ("set messages from your boss to Priority 1 … so they'll appear at the top of your In Box", LM ch. 5/§A-5).
- After-read/send/reply filing rules: "After reading a message / After sending a message / After replying to a message" pop-ups choose destination folders; automatic **retention** — permanently delete read mail, sent mail, and Deleted Mail after a configurable number of days (LM §A-2).
- Read/unread, replied flags; contextual menu "Mark as Read" (2.0v3, via Contextual Menu Manager); per-recipient send status: clicking a recipient's green check → "Mark recipient as unread" (per the readme — the effect is re-queuing that one recipient) ([2.0v2 readme](http://download.info.applefritter.com/Apple_Support_Area/Apple_Software_Updates/English-North_American/Macintosh/Application_Updates/Claris_Emailer/Claris_Emailer_2.x/Emailer2.0to2.0v2__Info.txt); [CEMH version page](https://web.archive.org/web/1999/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/version.shtml)).
- Keyboard navigation: Cmd-[ / Cmd-] previous/next message; Cmd-Opt-[ / ] delete-and-move prev/next; Cmd-D delete; **Cmd-Opt-F opens the file-message dialog — type the folder name, Return to file**; Copy Entire Message = Cmd-Opt-C (headers + body; long headers via Mail > Show Long Headers); recipient type shortcuts T/C/B for To/CC/BCC; hold Cmd to reveal button shortcuts (LM appendix; [CEMH shortcuts](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/shortcuts.shtml); [TidBITS 2000](https://tidbits.com/2000/04/24/switching-from-emailer-to-eudora-in-leaps-and-bounds/)).

**Composition and addressing**
- Type-ahead addressing from the Address Book; "+" adds a recipient row; magnifying-glass icons open find-address / find-file dialogs; each recipient gets a **destination-service pop-up** (which account/service carries it) and a To/CC/BCC type ([CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml); LM ch. 3).
- The **"Send via"** pop-up picked the sending account; two accounts could share one POP mailbox to give two identities ([CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- Address translation per service — e.g., typing a CompuServe `12345,2222` auto-converts the comma to a period for internet routing unless CompuServe is the selected destination ([CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- Enclosures: drag files in, drag a file onto the Emailer icon to create an attachment (2.0v3); automatic compression/decompression via Aladdin StuffIt; selectable encodings (BinHex/UU/Base64-class Mac encodings; per-account service default for encoding); enclosures detached into a Downloads folder; delete via enclosure-pane trash icon (LM ch. 3; [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml); [Claris product page](https://web.archive.org/web/1997/http://www.claris.com/products/claris/emailer/emailer.html)).
- Signatures: multiple named signatures, per-account default, **random signatures** drawn from a "Random Signatures" folder of text files (2.0; [Low End Mac](https://lowendmac.com/hints/emailer.shtml); [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- Quoting: "Paste as Quotation", colored quote text, and a configurable **attribution line** ("On 02/08/98 5:45 PM (-0500), Don Juan (djuan@earthlink.net) wrote:") via Replies prefs (LM; [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- Spell checker added in 2.0 — and it **skips quoted text** unless explicitly selected ([Claris product page](https://web.archive.org/web/1997/http://www.claris.com/products/claris/emailer/emailer.html); [CEMH version page](https://web.archive.org/web/1999/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/version.shtml)).
- Redirect (bounce-as-received) added in 2.0v3 — distinct from Forward ([CEMH version page](https://web.archive.org/web/1999/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/version.shtml)).
- URLs: Cmd-click a URL to open via Internet Config helpers; drag URLs into messages or into the Address Book; Address Book entries could be converted to URLs (LM "Working with URLs" appendix; [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)). Emailer was among the first apps to support **Internet Config** — shared, system-wide internet preferences ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claris_Emailer); [TidBITS "Ships"](https://tidbits.com/1995/07/31/claris-emailer-ships/)).

**Address Book**
- One record, multiple addresses **across different services**; groups (mailing lists); type-to-filter field; import/export tab-delimited text; 2.0v3 made it scriptable, linked group membership to contacts (edit contact → groups update), hideable contacts, duplicate-name detection on quick-add, sortable columns, group import/export (LM ch. 4; [CEMH version page](https://web.archive.org/web/1999/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/version.shtml)).

**Mail Actions (rules)**
- Triggered on incoming mail; criteria on From, To, CC, Reply-To, *any recipient*, Subject, body text, account, service — or "all mail"; operators: is/isn't, begins/ends with, contains/doesn't contain, **is/isn't in Address Book**, **is/isn't in Group [name]** (LM ch. 5).
- Actions: **set priority**, **file to folder**, **auto-reply** (with edited reply text), **auto-forward**; optional per-message log entry to the Auto File Log (LM ch. 5; [CEMH Mail Actions](https://web.archive.org/web/1999/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/mailactions.shtml)).
- Ordered evaluation — dragging actions set priority of evaluation; each action could stop lower actions; criteria could negate ("If true: do NOT apply actions"); 2.0 allowed up to 20 criteria and unlimited actions per Mail Action ([CEMH](https://web.archive.org/web/1999/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/mailactions.shtml); [Claris product page](https://web.archive.org/web/1997/http://www.claris.com/products/claris/emailer/emailer.html)).
- Safety valve: preference "**Do not automatically reply to mailing lists**" (suppressed auto-replies when a `Precedence:` header indicated list mail — LM-era RFC 2076-era practice) ([CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- Anti-spam Mail Action sets shipped by Fog City itself ([TidBITS review](https://tidbits.com/1997/06/02/email-reliance-emailer-2-0/)).
- 2.0v2 added the AppleScript `filter` verb — re-run Mail Actions on stored messages ([readme](http://download.info.applefritter.com/Apple_Support_Area/Apple_Software_Updates/English-North_American/Macintosh/Application_Updates/Claris_Emailer/Claris_Emailer_2.x/Emailer2.0to2.0v2__Info.txt)).

**Scriptability and integration**
- AppleScript support from v1.0v2; 2.0 added a full dictionary + in-app AppleScript menu; shipped scripts: mark read/unread, run mail actions on selection, toggle schedules, save messages as text, USENET WWW search, register/home-page links; `filter` verb, 255-char subject access, `properties` (2.0v2) ([Fog City utilities](http://www.fogcity.com/em_utilities.html); [2.0v2 readme](http://download.info.applefritter.com/Apple_Support_Area/Apple_Software_Updates/English-North_American/Macintosh/Application_Updates/Claris_Emailer/Claris_Emailer_2.x/Emailer2.0to2.0v2__Info.txt)).
- Community library: Import/Export 3.1 (Eudora ↔ Emailer, Unix mbox export), Convert Emailer1, AOL 3.0 address converter, FileMaker archive scripts, Emailer↔BBEdit editors, PGP integration, bulk forward, reply-in-same-folder, hard-wrap, QP decoders, EmailTools OneClick palette ([Fog City utilities 2.0](http://www.fogcity.com/em_utilities2.0.html); [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- Printing font settable only via AppleScript until 2.0v3 made monospaced the default ([CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- Preferences could live next to the app instead of System Folder:Claris → portable installs and multiple simultaneous copies; F5 at launch re-points the Emailer Files folder ([CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- Notifications: tone, flashing menu-bar mail icon, Log entries; connection error reports filed to In Box/Logs (LM §A-2; troubleshooting).

**MIME (2.0v3, per Dan Crevier via CEMH)**: proper MIME digest decoding, multipart/alternative → display the text part and discard HTML, consistent hard wrapping, Internet Config-based enclosure naming, correct outgoing part types, QP enclosures ([CEMH version page](https://web.archive.org/web/1999/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/version.shtml)). Note the era: Emailer deliberately stayed a **plain-text** client — it could not display styled/HTML mail ([TidBITS 2000](https://tidbits.com/2000/04/24/switching-from-emailer-to-eudora-in-leaps-and-bounds/)).

### 2.3 What users loved

- **One client for everything.** TidBITS 1995: the killer feature was handling *multiple services* — one phone call (or a few in one session) fetched all your mail into one place ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/1995/07/31/claris-emailer-ships/)). Wired 1996: "Emailer is more powerful and sophisticated than Eudora — perhaps its closest competitor"; the unified fetch was "a time and money saver" because you stopped browsing the online services themselves ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/1996/02/all-in-one-e-mail/)).
- **The filters.** Adam Engst, 1995: Emailer's filtering "— mostly thanks to the auto-forward and auto-reply features — are perhaps the best I've seen" ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/1995/07/31/claris-emailer-ships/)). Beta tester David Dean, 2007: "Emailer's filters couldn't be beat. Knowing which email address to use based upon the recipient's email. All inboxes integrated together as one. Filing a message away after you read it automatically. Filing a message directly from its open window. The keyboard navigation was perfect. The Scripting was fantastic." ([recollection](https://web.archive.org/web/20080624084550/http://blog.entourage.mvps.org/2007/05/in_the_beginning.html)).
- **Keyboard workflow.** Jeff Carlson stuck with Emailer until 2000; his #1 missed feature in Eudora: "highlight a message, press Command-Option-F, then begin typing the name of the folder… and hit Return" ([TidBITS 2000](https://tidbits.com/2000/04/24/switching-from-emailer-to-eudora-in-leaps-and-bounds/)).
- **The Address Book.** Carlson 1997: "Without a doubt, Emailer's Address Book rates as one of its coolest features" — instant narrowing as you type ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/1997/06/02/email-reliance-emailer-2-0/)).
- **Reliability and feel.** Long-time user Don Brown: ~13 years of daily use, "I can't think of anything [that] ever went wrong… it's never crashed" — he kept it until 2008 ([blog](http://gettheflick.blogspot.com/2008/12/goodbye-faithful-servant.html)). TidBITS 1997: "a full-featured program with few shortcomings" ([review](https://tidbits.com/1997/06/02/email-reliance-emailer-2-0/)). Even in 2023 a TidBITS commenter pleaded "Bring back Claris em@iler" ([comments](https://tidbits.com/2023/03/08/microsoft-outlook-for-mac-now-free-with-strings-attached/)).
- **The community was part of the product.** Emailer-talk was so vibrant that a January 1998 moderation crackdown triggered a mass exodus and a new list within "a few dozen minutes" — immortalized in a Georgetown sociology paper ([Omar Shahine via Entourage blog](https://web.archive.org/web/20071102102848/http://blog.entourage.mvps.org/2007/05/a_day_in_the_life_of_emailerta.html)). Fog City hosted a free library of scripts/utilities that functioned as a de-facto extension ecosystem ([Fog City](http://www.fogcity.com/em_utilities2.0.html)).

### 2.4 What users disliked / limits

- **1.x storage waste.** Each message a Finder file: on a ~700 MB HFS partition a 500-byte message ate ~20 KB (one allocation block); "a design mistake" per Engst ([TidBITS 1995](https://tidbits.com/1995/07/31/claris-emailer-ships/)). Carlson's friend reclaimed ~85 MB moving to 2.0 ([TidBITS 1997](https://tidbits.com/1997/06/02/email-reliance-emailer-2-0/)).
- **2.0 monolithic database.** A "large bull in a small china shop": syncing/backing up meant copying the entire ~87 MB file; corruption produced ghost "This message has been permanently deleted" entries and required the Option-launch rebuild ritual; community FAQ documented typical vs advanced rebuilds ([TidBITS 2000](https://tidbits.com/2000/04/24/switching-from-emailer-to-eudora-in-leaps-and-bounds/); [CEMH corruption FAQ](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/most.shtml); [2.0v2 readme](http://download.info.applefritter.com/Apple_Support_Area/Apple_Software_Updates/English-North_American/Macintosh/Application_Updates/Claris_Emailer/Claris_Emailer_2.x/Emailer2.0to2.0v2__Info.txt)).
- **Exit cost.** Proprietary store → no native export; leaving required the "Export – Eudora" AppleScript and tab-delimited address dumps, and converting thousands of messages was slow and occasionally hung ([TidBITS 2000](https://tidbits.com/2000/04/24/switching-from-emailer-to-eudora-in-leaps-and-bounds/)).
- **Slow full-text search and big folders.** The 2.0 database "didn't pass my coffee test" for all-folder search; folders slowed as they filled (in-memory folder-list cache) ([TidBITS 2000](https://tidbits.com/2000/04/24/switching-from-emailer-to-eudora-in-leaps-and-bounds/); [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- **Usability gaps.** Wired: less intuitive than Eudora; 1.0 instability (froze at startup, two reinstalls in 10 days); no Prodigy/Delphi; no bundled CompuServe TCP tool ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/1996/02/all-in-one-e-mail/)). TidBITS 1997 nits: no redirect (added only in 2.0v3), group entries didn't follow contact edits (fixed 2.0v3), Cmd-D oddity on log views, no multi-message save-to-text ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/1997/06/02/email-reliance-emailer-2-0/)).
- **Protocol-era quirks.** Dropped connections left duplicates on the server; UIDL-based "leave on server" could misfire against buggy POP servers; a 1.1 MB spam with thousands of recipients froze the unpack phase; unpacking itself could take minutes when heavy Mail Actions ran ([CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).
- **No HTML mail.** By 2000 this had flipped from feature (deliberate plain-text purity — 2.0v3 even discarded the HTML half of multipart/alternative) to liability: users "waded through the HTML tags" ([CEMH](https://web.archive.org/web/1999/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/version.shtml); [TidBITS 2000](https://tidbits.com/2000/04/24/switching-from-emailer-to-eudora-in-leaps-and-bounds/)).
- **Dead product.** No OS X port, no source release, no successor; PowerMail positioned itself as "a viable Emailer replacement" ([Entourage blog](https://web.archive.org/web/20080624084550/http://blog.entourage.mvps.org/2007/05/in_the_beginning.html); [Low End Mac](https://lowendmac.com/1998/can-apple-save-emailer-by-giving-it-away/)).
- **Privacy/encryption**: no encryption or signing built in (PGP only via community scripts); no local password protection of stored mail ([Fog City utilities](http://www.fogcity.com/em_utilities2.0.html); [CEMH tips](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)).

### 2.5 Lost features — Emailer vs. today's mainstream clients

Status of modern clients verified 2026-10-11: Mimestream is a Gmail-API-only macOS client, $49.99/yr subscription ([pricing](https://mimestream.com/pricing/); note Google announced project-wide Gmail API usage above a basic allowance will be billed later in 2026 — same page). Spark (Readdle) is cross-platform with unified Smart Inbox; paid tiers are AI-centric ($99–199/yr) ([Spark pricing](https://sparkmailapp.com/pricing)). Thunderbird remains actively developed under MZLA; it shipped IMAP/POP/EWS and is reaching **Microsoft Graph** parity for Exchange Online in 2026 ([June 2026 dev digest](https://blog.thunderbird.net/2026/06/thunderbird-monthly-development-june-2026/); roadmap: [blog.thunderbird.net](https://blog.thunderbird.net/2026/03/introducing-our-public-roadmaps/)).

| Emailer feature | Status in 2026 mainstream clients |
|---|---|
| Unified inbox across *different service types* (POP3 + AOL + CompuServe + wireless) | Partially: all clients unify IMAP accounts; only Thunderbird (+Outlook) span protocol families (IMAP/EWS/Graph). Mimestream = Gmail only. Nobody abstracts over non-mail messaging silos. |
| Offline-first compose + scheduled batch connections | Weakened: modern clients assume always-online. Scheduled *send* exists (Gmail, Outlook, Spark, Mail since Ventura); scheduled *fetch* does not. True queue-and-flush batching is gone. |
| Reviewable Out Box with per-recipient send status and "mark unread"→resend | **Gone.** No mainstream client exposes per-recipient delivery state or per-person resend. |
| Send Now = just this message vs. flush queue | Gone — Send is immediate (Undo Send gives seconds). |
| Mail Actions: order+stop, group/address-book criteria, set-priority, auto-reply w/ template, auto-forward | Partial: Mail rules (color/flag/move/reply/forward/redirect, "sender in contacts", stop-evaluating) and Gmail filters (skip/archive/label/forward; no per-filter reply templates, no group criteria); Thunderbird filters are closest (tags, importance, junk, forward, reply-with-template). None match the full ordered, group-aware set with Emailer's ergonomics. |
| Auto File Log (audit view of what rules filed where) | **Gone.** Nothing equivalent; closest is Thunderbird's filter log or Gmail "filters" list (not an audit trail). |
| Auto-file after read/send/reply | **Gone** as a built-in preference; approximated by rules and send-and-archive extensions. |
| Retention: auto-delete read/sent/deleted after N days | Mostly gone client-side: Mail/Outlook can auto-empty Trash/Junk; per-folder aging of read/sent mail is absent (Gmail does it server-side via retention labels only in Workspace). |
| Named + colored priorities (a label system) | Transmuted: Gmail labels, Outlook categories, Thunderbird tags+importance. Custom *named* priorities are gone. |
| Cmd-Opt-F type-to-file keyboard filing | Weakened: Mail predicts destinations; Mimestream/Spark have move shortcuts; quick-fuzzy-filing is not standard (Mail Act On/MailButler historically filled it). |
| Multiple signatures + random signature | Multiple: yes everywhere. **Random: effectively gone** (Thunderbird add-ons aside). |
| Configurable attribution line | Gone — fixed "On … wrote:" formats. |
| Drag-anything-to-anything (files→icon to attach, URLs→address book, addresses→fields) | Partial at best. |
| Internet Config shared internet prefs | Transmuted: macOS Internet Accounts covers some accounts; mailto: handler is per-user. |
| Location profiles for connections | Transmuted: Focus modes + Mimestream per-account notification schedules; network-aware fetch profiles don't exist. |
| Partial retrieve / preview big mail before downloading | Mostly internal now (IMAP partial fetch, remote-content blocking); not a user-facing "see it, then decide" feature. |
| "Get all messages from server" / resync escape hatch | Rarely exposed; account rebuilds are hidden ops. |
| Auto-extract enclosures to a folder | Uncommon (Thunderbird add-ons, Mail rules+scripts). |
| First-class scriptability (AppleScript dictionary + script menu + shipped library) | Weakened: Mail keeps a dictionary but the ecosystem died; Thunderbird has WebExtension add-ons; Mimestream/Spark have none. Nothing matches Fog City's hosted script library + community. |

### 2.6 Re-imagining for 2026 (for Sweetbriar's IMAP/JMAP/Exchange/Gmail/Proton world)

| Emailer idea | 2026 form | Client-side or server-dependent |
|---|---|---|
| "Services" abstraction | **Backend adapter layer** in the Rust core: one normalized account/mailbox/message model over IMAP (+IDLE), JMAP (RFC 8620/8621), Microsoft Graph, Gmail API, Proton Bridge (local IMAP). This is Emailer's soul and Sweetbriar's stated mission. | Both: normalization is client-side; capability surface is server-dependent (e.g., JMAP push vs IMAP polling). |
| Connection = batch sync | Sync engine with an explicit sync now / sync profile concept; per-account fetch policies; per-network/per-Focus profiles as the modern "Locations". | Client-side orchestration; push comes from IMAP IDLE, JMAP EventSource/WebSocket (RFC 8887), Graph webhooks/delta, Gmail `history.list` + `watch`. |
| Out Box queue | Local **commit-log outbox**: messages staged with per-recipient state (queued/sent/failed), resend-to-one-recipient, "send only this" vs "flush". Server-side scheduled send where it exists (Graph `deferredDeliveryTime`, Gmail scheduled send, SMTP FUTURERELEASE RFC 4865, JMAP `EmailSubmission` sendAt-style controls). | Client-side core; optional server assist. |
| Mail Actions | Local rules engine: ordered, stop-able; criteria incl. contact/group membership, list detection; actions = set label/priority, file, notify, auto-reply w/ template, auto-forward; run-on-demand (`filter` verb equivalent) on stored mail. Optional **sync to server rules**: Sieve via ManageSieve (RFC 5804) for Fastmail/Cyrus/Stalwart; Gmail filters API; Graph `mailFolders/inbox/messageRules`. | Client-side baseline; per-backend server push as a per-account option with clear ordering semantics. |
| Auto-reply/forward safety | Honor `List-Id`/`List-Unsubscribe`/`Precedence: list`/`Auto-Submitted` (RFC 2369, 3834, 2919), set `Auto-Submitted: auto-replied`, rate-limit per sender, never reply to `MAILER-DAEMON`. | Client-side. |
| Auto File Log | Append-only **rule-audit view** in the DB (message → rule → action → folder, with undo). Trivial to build on SQLite; huge trust win. | Client-side. |
| Named + colored priorities | Local labels with names/colors; **sync as IMAP keywords** (`$Label1`–`$Label5`-style or custom) and JMAP `$keywords` so they survive across clients; map to Gmail labels via the API backend. | Both — keywords propagate through IMAP/JMAP; purely local otherwise. |
| Auto-file after read/send/reply | Per-folder workflow rules ("after reading → move to …"), including file-reply-with-original; execute via IMAP `MOVE` (RFC 6851) / JMAP `Email/set` mailbox moves. | Client-side trigger; server executes move natively. |
| Retention policies | Per-folder "keep read/sent N days" sweeper with tombstoned deletes; sync expunge carefully; surface in a settings pane, never silent. | Client-side; server may enforce its own retention — document interactions. |
| Keyboard filing | Fuzzy file-dialog (Cmd-Opt-K-style "Move to…"), learns from prior filings per sender/list. | Client-side. |
| Partial retrieve / preview | `BODY.PEEK[HEADER]` + `BODYSTRUCTURE` to show size/attachment manifest before download; "download on demand" per-part; JMAP equivalent via `Email/get` + blob download. | Client-side UX over standard protocol features. |
| Address book w/ groups + multiple service addresses | Local contacts DB synced via CardDAV; per-contact routing rules (choose From/identity by recipient domain/address — the feature David Dean missed). | Client-side routing; CardDAV sync. |
| Scriptability | AppleScript dictionary (nostalgia cohort expects it) + **App Intents/Shortcuts** + a local CLI (and/or HTTP API); ship a community script repo like Fog City did. | Client-side. |
| Signatures incl. random | Multiple per account + per-recipient rules + optional random/rotating pool. | Client-side. |
| Sounds/menu extras | Notification Center + Focus filters; custom per-rule sounds. | Client-side. |
| "Get all messages from server" | Explicit "Resync mailbox" escape hatch (UIDVALIDITY-safe). | Both. |
| Auto-extract enclosures | Optional "save attachments to folder" action w/ deterministic naming. | Client-side. |
| Storage | Do **not** repeat either Emailer mistake: use SQLite (WAL) for headers/metadata + FTS5 (or Tantivy) for bodies + content-addressed attachment store; incremental backup-friendly; **standard export (mbox/EML/JMAP dump) built in from day one** so Sweetbriar never becomes the next Emailer exit-cost story. | Client-side. |
| Proprietary-service plumbing (AOL/CompuServe/RadioMail CCL scripts) | Obsolete — replaced by the adapter layer + provider-specific auth flows (OAuth2/OIDC, app passwords). | Server-dependent. |

## 3. Options compared

**Storage architecture** (the defining Emailer trade-off):

| Option | Emailer precedent | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| File-per-message (Maildir/EML) | Emailer 1.x | Standard, greppable, incremental backup, easy export | Slower folder scans at scale; index needed anyway |
| Monolithic DB | Emailer 2.0 (Mail Database+Index) | Fast, compact | Corruption blast radius, all-or-nothing backup, lock-in — Emailer's #2 regret |
| SQLite (WAL) + blob store + FTS index | — (Sweetbriar candidate) | Compact, transactional, single-file backups, fast search, recoverable | Still one big file for messages — mitigate with export + integrity checks + replicas |

**Rules engine placement:**

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Client-only rules | Works on every backend incl. Gmail API-less IMAP; offline | Only runs when client runs (Emailer's exact limitation: scheduled connections needed the app open) |
| Server-only (Sieve/Gmail filters/Graph rules) | Runs always, fast | Feature ceiling varies; hard to unify UX |
| **Hybrid (recommended)**: client engine + optional push-to-server per account | Best of both; Emailer parity everywhere, better where server allows | Ordering semantics must be explicit ("server rules run first, then client rules on sync") |

**Scriptability surface:**

| Option | Reach | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| AppleScript dictionary | The Emailer demographic literally | Cheap-ish to add late, painful to retrofit |
| Shortcuts/App Intents | Modern macOS-native automation | Apple's current direction |
| CLI + local HTTP/RPC API | Cross-platform (Linux UI later), testable | Best long-term investment for Sweetbriar's shared core |

**Exchange backend (as of 2026-10-11):**

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| EWS | **Don't build for Exchange Online** — blocking started Oct 1, 2026, permanent by Apr 2027 ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/exchange/clients-and-mobile-in-exchange-online/deprecation-of-ews-exchange-online)); only relevant for on-prem Exchange Server, where it remains supported |
| Microsoft Graph | Required for M365; Thunderbird reached near-parity mid-2026 — precedent + cautionary scope guide ([TB digest](https://blog.thunderbird.net/2026/06/thunderbird-monthly-development-june-2026/)) |

## 4. Recommendations for Sweetbriar

**Steal these, in priority order:**

1. **The unified service abstraction** — Emailer's founding insight is Sweetbriar's founding mission. The difference: Emailer's "services" were CCL scripts over proprietary networks; Sweetbriar's are protocol/API adapters (IMAP+IDLE, JMAP, Graph, Gmail API, Proton Bridge) over one normalized message model. Keep the user-facing simplicity — one In Box, one Out Box, one Address Book, one rule engine — not the plumbing.
2. **The Out Box as a first-class, inspectable queue.** Per-message and per-recipient state, "send this one now" vs "flush everything", send-later, and mark-unsent resend. Nobody offers this in 2026; it also gives you draft/queue guarantees for free in the data-safety story.
3. **Mail Actions as a hybrid rules engine** — ordered rules with stop semantics, group/contact criteria, set-priority/label, file, notify, auto-reply (templated, `Auto-Submitted`-compliant), auto-forward; run-on-demand over stored mail (the `filter` verb). Push to Sieve/Gmail filters/Graph rules per account as an opt-in "run on server" toggle. Auto File Log = the audit/undo view, from day one.
4. **Workflow automation, not just filtering:** auto-file after read/send/reply, file-reply-with-original, per-folder retention. These are small features that produce the "it thinks the way I do" devotion Emailer inspired.
5. **Keyboard-first everything**: Cmd-Opt-F-style fuzzy filing, prev/next and delete-and-next, recipient-type shortcuts, discoverable shortcut overlay (Emailer showed button shortcuts when you held Cmd — a lovely, cheap trick).
6. **Named, colored priority labels** synced via IMAP keywords/JMAP `$keywords` — gives you Emailer's priorities *and* interoperable labels.
7. **A real script ecosystem**: AppleScript dictionary + App Intents + CLI/local API; ship a curated script repo and document "how Emailer users did X" recipes (the Fog City utilities page is the template).
8. **Partial-download preview and a visible "resync" escape hatch** — cheap to build over BODYSTRUCTURE/UIDVALIDITY, and they make the app feel trustworthy on bad networks.
9. **Small delights**: random signatures, configurable attribution line, per-connection status window showing *what* is being fetched (Emailer showed the subject mid-download — a tiny window into the machine that made waiting tolerable).

**Trade-offs to accept consciously:** client rules only run when Sweetbriar runs (mitigate with server-rule sync where offered); an inspectable queue costs you "instant send" UX (offer Undo-Send-style grace instead); labels-as-keywords leak a private label scheme to other clients (acceptable — it's the interop mechanism).

**Things Emailer did that you should NOT copy:** monolithic opaque storage with a rebuild ritual; modal connection dialogs that block the app; per-service address translation (era-specific); and the exit-cost failure — Emailer's community *had* to write extraction scripts. Ship mbox/EML export on day one.

**Strongest counter-argument:** much of Emailer's genius was *context-bound* — it solved 1995 problems (per-minute dial-up billing, five incompatible siloed mail networks, 14.4 kbps attachments). In 2026 the batch-connection model, location dial profiles, partial-retrieve economics, and unified-proprietary-inbox are obsolete or already commoditized (every client has a unified IMAP inbox; scheduled send is table stakes). And Emailer objectively *lost*: Eudora outlived it, and its successor lineage (Outlook Express → Entourage → Outlook) is precisely what Sweetbriar is designed to be *better* than. The risk is spending depth budget re-enacting nostalgia instead of attacking 2026's actual pain — HTML mail, hundred-gigabyte mailboxes, mobile parity, OAuth/app-password churn, Gmail API quota billing, and spam. The counter to the counter: the specific things devotees still miss — ordered reply-capable rules, keyboard filing, file-after-read, queue inspection, scriptability, retention — are *not* context-bound; they're workflow primitives nobody rebuilt. Revive those, not the dial-up chrome.

## 5. Risks and open questions

- **Corruption credibility.** Sweetbriar's single biggest reputational risk is repeating 2.0's "my mail database is corrupt" story. SQLite+WAL+checksums+migrations+in-app export and scheduled local backups must land before scale.
- **Rule-engine conflicts.** If users run server rules (Gmail filters, Exchange rules) *and* Sweetbriar rules, ordering/duplication must be documented and inspectable (the Auto File Log helps).
- **Auto-reply/forward danger.** Backscatter, loops, and vacation-reply leaks to mailing lists are real; Emailer's own docs warned "set vacation mail actions carefully" ([CEMH](https://web.archive.org/web/2000/http://www.macemail.com/emailer/CEMH/tips_and_highlights.shtml)). Need rate limits, `Auto-Submitted` headers, list detection, loop breakers.
- **Gmail API economics.** Google will bill project-wide Gmail API usage above a basic allowance starting later in 2026 ([Mimestream's disclosure](https://mimestream.com/pricing/subscription-licensing), verified 2026-10-11). A free client with many users could hit real costs; prefer IMAP for Gmail, or budget the API quota deliberately.
- **JMAP reach.** Still mostly Fastmail/Cyrus/Stalwart in 2026 — support it, but don't bet the UX on JMAP-only features.
- **Proton.** Access is via local Bridge (IMAP/SMTP) on paid tiers — treat as an IMAP source with Bridge lifecycle edge cases (unverified details beyond Bridge model; check current Proton Bridge terms before implementation).
- **AppleScript vs Shortcuts priority.** The nostalgic cohort wants AppleScript; macOS wants App Intents. Probably ship App Intents first for Shortcuts coverage, then a dictionary — but this needs a product call.
- **Multi-account identity.** Emailer's "choose identity by recipient" needs care under OAuth-scoped sendAs permissions (Gmail sendAs aliases, Graph sendAs rights); worst case is a bounced send.
- **Migration in.** Import from mbox/EML/Mail/Thunderbird is table stakes. *Fantasy feature:* an Emailer database importer for the remaining faithful — tiny market, enormous goodwill; feasibility unverified (2.0 database format undocumented).
- **What happened to the code.** Apple's ownership post-1998 is clear (Apple owns Emailer per Fog City), but whether any source survives internally is unknown; no evidence of a leak or open-source release ever occurred.
- **Conflicting dates.** The Entourage blog's "Claris purchase May 1996 / 1.0 June 1996" conflicts with contemporaneous TidBITS (licensed April 1995, shipped July 1995); Wikipedia's Jan 22, 1998 certification date vs Apple's posted June 9, 1998 updater date — reconciled above as certification vs posting, but the certification date rests on Wikipedia's secondary claim.
