**(a) GOA OAuth reuse:** I support a documented GNOME agreement as a release gate. GNOME asks third-party maintainers to contact its maintainers/design/release teams and preserve application identity; it does not prescribe a written agreement. The revision correctly distinguishes policy from Sweetbriar's stricter choice. [GNOME goals](https://gnome.pages.gitlab.gnome.org/gnome-online-accounts/goals.html).

My recommendation is to document the provider-policy basis as part of that agreement: GNOME's permission alone cannot establish Google's approval of a particular commercial consumer. Accurate branding and restricted-scope verification remain Google's requirements. Whether this specific arrangement is permitted remains **unverified**. Keep Sweetbriar's own authorization path available. [Google OAuth policy](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/policies).

The strongest counter-argument is practical: GOA publishes a consumer API, and Geary already consumes its tokens. A bespoke agreement can delay a useful native integration even when the technical path works. That justifies a bounded integration experiment while the production gate remains in place. [GOA consumer guide](https://gnome.pages.gitlab.gnome.org/gnome-online-accounts/overview.html); [Geary mediator](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GNOME/geary/main/src/client/application/goa-mediator.vala).

**(b) Compose cue:** I favor the opaque identity strip as the default, with the full From address and a unique textual cue. A controlled opaque surface makes contrast verification more predictable. Color must supplement another distinguishing cue; the revised design does that. [WCAG use of color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html). Apple's material guidance also cautions that settings can change an effect's apparent color. [Apple Materials HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials).

The strongest counter-argument is perceptual: a restrained strip may be less conspicuous than a tinted window, especially in inactive windows and thumbnails. Keep Strong mode and use recognition tests to set the default's prominence. This is a design judgment, not a demonstrated reduction in wrong-identity sends. The full-screen visibility recipe is correct according to Apple's `fullScreenMinHeight` contract. [Apple accessory documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nstitlebaraccessoryviewcontroller/fullscreenminheight).
